## Post 3 — Video Post #2 (Vin Thomas / Fixel — recurring revenue)

**Format:** Native video upload, no link
**Recommended clip:** Vin Thomas (Fixel) | 14:52–17:48 — "I had this idea... clients just needed a website and never wanted to talk to me again... That was an incorrect assumption."
**Goal:** Views and engagement

---

"I assumed clients just wanted a website and never wanted to talk to me again."

Vin Thomas ran his agency for years with that assumption. Build it. Hand it off. Move on.

Then his clients started asking to stay.

Not because something broke. Because they wanted a long-term partner — someone who understood their business and could help it evolve.

That shift — from project-based to recurring — changed everything about how Fixel operates. Revenue got steadier. The team could plan ahead. And the client relationships actually got better.

If you're still treating every project as a one-and-done, you might be leaving the most valuable part of the relationship on the table.

## Post 4 — Engagement Post (niching)

**Format:** Text only, no link
**Goal:** Comments and conversation

---

Hot take: Most agencies don't have a growth problem. They have a focus problem.

I've spent [X years / a lot of time] working with WordPress agencies, and the pattern is hard to miss.

The ones growing fastest usually do fewer things — not more.

They pick a niche. They get known for it. Referrals get easier because people know exactly who to send. Sales cycles shrink because prospects immediately see themselves in the work.

Meanwhile, the "full-service" agencies are constantly re-explaining what they do and competing on price with everyone.

It sounds counterintuitive. Narrowing your focus to grow faster.

But I keep seeing it play out the same way.

Curious — if you run an agency, did you choose your niche deliberately or did it find you?

## Post 5 — Engagement Post (pricing fear)

**Format:** Text only, no link
**Goal:** Comments and conversation

---

The most expensive mistake I see agencies make isn't a bad hire or a lost client.

It's waiting too long to raise prices.

I hear it constantly:

"We're worried clients will leave."
"The market won't support it."
"We'll raise them next quarter."

And then next quarter becomes next year.

Meanwhile, margins are getting tighter. The team is stretched. You're doing $20K work for $8K because the rate was set three years ago and no one wanted to have the uncomfortable conversation.

Here's the thing — in almost every case I've seen, clients don't push back the way agencies expect. The ones who leave over a fair price increase were probably the wrong clients anyway.

The risk isn't raising prices. It's staying where you are.

Anyone else been through this? How did you handle it?

## Notes for the sales team

- **Personalize the bracketed sections** — add your own experience, tenure, or perspective
- **First line is everything** — LinkedIn truncates after ~2-3 lines on mobile. If the hook doesn't stop the scroll, nothing else matters
- **Don't add hashtags** — they signal "marketing post" and reduce engagement on personal profiles
- **Post timing** — Tuesday through Thursday, 8-10am in your audience's timezone tends to perform best
- **Engage after posting** — reply to every comment in the first 2 hours. The algorithm rewards early engagement heavily
- **For video posts** — upload the clip natively to LinkedIn (don't paste a YouTube link). Native video gets dramatically more reach
