Hot take Most agencies don't have a growth problem, they have a focus problem. I've spent [X years / a lot of time] working with WordPress agencies and the pattern is impossible to miss. The ones growing fastest usually do fewer things, not more. They pick a niche, get known for it, and then something shifts: → Referrals flow because people know exactly who to send → Sales cycles shrink because prospects see themselves in the work → Positioning gets easier because you're not competing with everyone Meanwhile the "full-service" agencies are constantly re-explaining what they do and competing on price. Narrowing your focus to grow faster sounds counterintuitive, but I keep seeing it play out the same way. If you run an agency, did you choose your niche deliberately or did it find you? Nobody talks about this at events but it might be the most underrated growth lever there is You hire great people. You land the right clients. You build systems that work. And then your hosting goes down on a Tuesday afternoon and suddenly you're firefighting instead of leading. Vin Thomas from Fixel put it perfectly: his whole day had become reacting to fires instead of being strategic. That made me realize something about the agencies I work with at Kinsta that run the smoothest. They've been intentional about one thing most people treat as an afterthought: Their infrastructure partner. Not just "where the sites live" but who picks up the phone when something breaks, whether staging actually matches production, and if the support team understands WordPress deeply enough to solve real problems. It's not glamorous, but it's the difference between spending your Tuesday on strategy or spending it on damage control.
